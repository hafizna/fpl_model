Strategic briefing · internal

Keep the model. Change the cadence.

You asked whether to replace the projection model or change how you play. The honest answer is neither is the real question — the model is sound, and most of the rigidity you feel lives in the decision policy wrapped around it, not the projections themselves. Two of the three things you need are already half-built in the uncommitted work.

Context: rank ~1M, outperforming template through GW3, zero transfersIntent: Wildcard soon, then roll 3 GW to bank free transfersHorizon: 5 Gameweeks

01The verdict

Do not switch models. The Benchwarmers component base is an explainable projection engine — every xPts number decomposes into eleven auditable pieces (appearance, goals, assists, clean sheet, saves, cards, bonus, defensive contribution, and the fixture adjustments). That is a feature for projection and it is worth keeping. It is a liability only if you let its author's low-transfer, pattern-hunting style leak into your cadence. It has been leaking. That is fixable without touching the model.

Three moves, in priority order: (1) finish and run the in-season v2 update so the model can actually see the players you bought this season; (2) switch to a weekly review at a five-Gameweek horizon — which is exactly what your Wildcard plan needs anyway; (3) express "I will take variance to beat template" as a decision objective, never as a tweak to the xPts numbers.

02Two layers, and only one of them is rigid

The projection layer answers "how many points will this player score?" The decision layer answers "so what should I do?" They are different code and different problems.

Hard truth · 01The model was never the thing stopping you from being aggressive. A policy that says "one move, review in three weeks" was.

03What the v2 update actually fixes

Until this week, the in-season model scored every Gameweek off last season's per-90 rates.

Every in-season projection carried the flag FROZEN_PREVIOUS_SEASON_PLAYER_RATES. A player's 2026/27 form — their actual xG, xA, defensive contributions, saves across GW1–GW3 — never updated their rate inputs. That is the single biggest reason the model cannot "see" the new signings you bought on judgement: to the model, a player with no Premier League history last season is still a near-blank.

In-season baseline v2 closes exactly this. It blends each player's current-season rate toward their prior with small-sample shrinkage (900 prior minutes — ten full matches — so one good game does not swing the projection). New signings with no usable history shrink toward the position-and-price cohort prior instead of getting their raw one-match rate. The historical prior is pinned by ID and the final event IDs are hashed into the run identity, so the update is fully auditable and old v1 projections are never rewritten.

Status · needs your sign-off to ship

The v2 code and the GW4–GW8 model runs already exist in the local database. The published web/release.json has now been regenerated from them (5-Gameweek, inseason_baseline_v2, health: shadow). It is still a research/shadow release — implementing an adaptive rate policy does not by itself prove it beats v1 out of sample. Treat v2 as a better-informed input, not a validated one.

04Your edge and the model's edge are different sizes

On the one honest backtest in the repo — a walk-forward replay of 2025/26, scoring each Gameweek from only its own prior weeks — the model beats a naive "just use each player's running average" predictor by:

  • MAE: ~0.019 points per player-fixture better (95% gameweek-cluster interval [0.006, 0.035] — excludes zero, so real, but small)
  • RMSE: ~0.064 better [0.042, 0.093] — it mostly helps by being less wrong on the big misses

That is a genuine but modest edge over the dumbest possible baseline. Meanwhile you are at rank ~1M, beating template on judgement, with no transfers. Read those two facts together:

Hard truth · 02Your player evaluation is the sharper instrument. The model's job is to be the disciplined second opinion — the thing that says "your gut likes this, here is where the components disagree and by how much" — not the thing that decides.

This also tells you how to use it: when the model and your judgement agree, move with confidence. When they disagree, that is the signal to slow down and work out which of you is seeing something the other is not — a fixture, a role change, a minutes risk.

05The cadence change

Adopt a weekly review at a five-Gameweek horizon. Keep the three-Gameweek wrapper only for backtest continuity.

The planner already supports this in the uncommitted work. Concretely:

  • Every week, refresh the release and re-score the next five Gameweeks — even when the planned action is "roll". A five-Gameweek decision window is a planning aid, not a commitment to ignore new evidence for five weeks.
  • Future Gameweeks use their own fixtures but freeze appearance, rate, and team-strength inputs to the anchor. Every future row is flagged as such. This is a projection of "if nothing else changes", which is why you re-run it weekly.
  • Two-transfer bundles are now evaluated where a single move checked in isolation would fail — a jointly funded upgrade, a club swap that briefly breaks the 3-per-club limit mid-transfer. The result is flagged APPROXIMATE_BUNDLE_SHORTLIST because it prunes to a proxy shortlist before exact scoring.

06This week: the Wildcard run

Your stated plan — two free transfers now, Wildcard soon, then roll three Gameweeks to bank up to five FT — is exactly the scenario the new compare_wildcard path was built for. It returns three fully server-scored paths over the published horizon:

  1. Hold — roll every week, bank the transfers.
  2. Use your free transfers — the best bounded roll / one-move / two-move path.
  3. Wildcard + roll — a Wildcard squad built for the five-Gameweek horizon, then a forced roll for three weeks, then free-transfer paths.

Wildcard affordability uses your actual bank plus real sale proceeds; keeping a player you own costs their sale value as opportunity cost and preserves their real purchase and selling prices afterward. No reset to £100m, no phantom sell-and-rebuy spread on players you keep.

Read this before you play the chip

The comparison assumes the Wildcard is available and does not price the option value of keeping it for a later week — an injury crisis, a fixture swing, a price collapse. A small modelled gain for playing it now is not a recommendation to play it. If the "Wildcard + roll" path only edges out "use your free transfers" by a point or two over five Gameweeks, hold the chip.

Set the run up like this

Horizon 5, Wildcard this Gameweek, roll_after_wildcard = 3. Start terminal_ft_value at 0, then raise it to 1 and 2 and watch whether the ranking between the three paths changes. If it does not, the decision is robust. If a small assumed value for banked transfers is what flips it, you are deciding on an assumption, not on points — treat that as "too close to call, use judgement".

07The one genuine concept gap: no ownership term

You are beating template because you diverge from it. A pure expected-points optimiser, run to convergence, will quietly pull you back toward template — maximising mean points and matching the crowd are nearly the same objective once everyone owns the same high-xPts players.

Hard truth · 03If you want the model to support your edge instead of eroding it, it needs an optional ownership-aware objective — and right now it does not have one.

The shape: rank moves not by raw xPts gain but by something like xPts_gain + λ · (xPts_above_replacement) · (1 − effective_ownership), as a toggle, never the default, with raw xPts always shown beside it. This is on the roadmap (Sprint 6, "ownership/EO objectives") but unbuilt. It is the highest-value new feature for a manager in your position — more than any projection refinement.

08What not to do

  • Do not switch to a black-box ML predictor. You would lose the eleven-component "why", and you do not have the multi-season prospective, labelled data to trust one. The repo's own principle — every extension must beat the baseline in backtesting — is correct.
  • Do not make the model "more aggressive" by tuning xPts. That destroys the audit trail and does not actually change your decisions in a controlled way. Aggression belongs in the objective (the terminal_ft_value knob, a risk-adjusted ranking with a negative uncertainty weight), where you can dial it and see it.
  • Do not hand projection authority to an LLM. Presentation layer or structured-input helper only.
  • Do not build the 12–19 Gameweek branching solver. You have a Wildcard decision this week. A bounded five-Gameweek comparison is the right scope; the season-long stochastic planner is a much larger project with much less marginal value.
